{% comment %} Pastpaper certificate — one A4 landscape sheet. **Recreated from the midterm certificate**, not invented. That card is the house design and a student who sits both should be handed the same object: white field, blue rail down the left carrying the shield and a vertical wordmark, the wide letterspaced blue title, the score in a filled disc with a navy subject pill under it, "Awarded to" and the name in heavy sans, the citation, a chip, and a two-up footer. The faint oversized shield watermark behind the content is the same device. Three things differ, and each is content rather than styling: * the rail reads PAST PAPER rather than MIDTERM; * there is no INSTRUCTOR block — a pastpaper is issued by the school on completion, not by a person, so printing a name would invent an author; * the rank chip becomes an accuracy chip, because a pastpaper is sat whenever the student likes and has no cohort to be ranked against. Written as a Django template with real variables rather than as another ~600 KB design export whose data is injected by walking text nodes for "Aziz Karimov". That technique exists only because those two files cannot be edited, and it needs a second injection map in the React viewer kept in step with the backend one. This needs neither. The error report is NOT on this sheet. The midterm keeps them as two documents — a certificate and a separate portrait report — and this follows, so the report can be drawn by the midterm's own renderer and the two sheets stay one family. See classes/pastpaper_certificate_pdf.py. Fonts are a system sans stack rather than an embedded webfont: the bundled templates ship a webfont that Chromium embeds as a Type-3 PDF font, whose advance widths the Linux font engine rounds into jammed-together words (see the --font-render-hinting note in certificate_html_pdf.py). A system stack cannot hit that. {% endcomment %}
